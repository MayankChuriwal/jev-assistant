Design spec · draft for your review

Jev brain and hands

Jev reads your chat with a seven-label classifier and lets a small model click inside one task at a time, so a vague request can only be guessed or dropped. This spec gives the conversation to a model with tools (the brain) and turns today's browser worker into those tools (the hands). Jev keeps choosing each next click on a page, and also judges your typed yes before a mandate is touched.

  • Read the list while it was still drawing: GOOGLE PLAY's card had blank Frequency, Executed and Valid till, and AISLE's card wasn't drawn yet.
  • Jev's done-check said yes (0.95) on a read that showed only the page header.

“No UPI AutoPay mandate for “aisle” subscription was found.”

AISLE was on the list 37 seconds earlier, and nothing was cancelled in between. It still exists.

The cause: the layers are the wrong way round

You described the model as the intelligence and Jev as the decision layer for the next action. What's built is the reverse:

  • Jev, which picks one of seven labels, plus regexes decide what each chat message means.
  • The model (gpt-4.1-mini, not 5.1-mini) works only inside one browser task. It sees your raw words as the goal, and one page. It never sees the conversation.
  • Every message starts a task from zero. Between messages Core keeps only a set of payee names (core.py:165).

So a vague request has nowhere to go. The task can guess (it pressed wrong cards earlier today) or end. I blocked the guessing, so it ends: that's the lockdown. Today's 34 fix commits were all symptoms of this one flaw.

Is Jev the issue?

It's in the wrong job. A label-picker can't hold a dialogue like “you have three, which one?”. Its right job is inside the browser: choosing the next click and checking the page, fast and cheap. It stays there, and gains one more yes-or-no job in the confirmation gate.

Is the model the issue?

Partly. gpt-4.1-mini made the random OPENAI pick and the early “done”. But a better model in the same seat would still be blind to the conversation. Swapping the model alone won't fix it; moving the model up to the conversation does.

5Message routing

Core.receive() decides in code, in this order. No model sees a captcha answer, a code, or a tap on a task's question.

flowchart TD
  A["A message arrives"] --> B{"A tap?"}
  B -->|on a brain reply's options| BI["Brain inbox: the option's full words"]
  B -->|on a task's question| QA["Answer that question"]
  B -->|no| C{"A task's question open?"}
  C -->|yes, and it's a stop word| ST["Cancel the task that asked"]
  C -->|yes| QA
  C -->|no| D{"Log out?"}
  D -->|yes| LO["End your kept sign-ins"]
  D -->|no| E{"A tool running?"}
  E -->|yes, a stop word| ST
  E -->|yes| QN["Queue it, say once: I'll get to that next"]
  E -->|no| BI
  • A task's question is one the hands asked: the captcha, the SMS code, the QR's two buttons, a final yes. Today Jev decided whether text answered it; now it does by rule, because only a running tool asks.
  • Stop words: stop, cancel, never mind, abort, ruko, bas. They cancel the task after its current step, as today.
  • Option taps carry an id; Core maps it back to the option's full words (a WhatsApp button shows only 20 characters).
  • The brain works one message at a time on its own thread. Typing shows while it thinks.

6The brain

Turn loop

flowchart TD
  IN["Next inbox message"] --> A["Add it to the conversation, typing on"]
  A --> M["Model step: instructions, tools, conversation, snapshot"]
  M -->|model error| ER["Say it couldn't, drop this turn's items"]
  M --> K{"Which tool?"}
  K -->|reply| RP["Send text and options, end the turn"]
  K -->|another tool| G{"Gate and guards"}
  G -->|rejected| RJ["Result: rejected, with a next hint"]
  G -->|needs your yes| CC["Code sends the confirmation card, end the turn"]
  G -->|pass| T["Run it: progress line, worker task, captured result"]
  RJ --> N{"Steps left?"}
  T --> N
  N -->|yes| M
  N -->|last step| MR["Model step, reply only"]
  MR --> RP
  • Up to 8 model steps a turn. Each step must call exactly one tool (tool_choice: required, no parallel calls). reply ends the turn; the last step offers only reply.
  • No time limit on a turn: a tool may wait minutes for you to type a code. The step cap bounds the model, not you.
  • If a model call fails, the turn's items are dropped so the conversation stays valid, and you get one plain line saying so.

Instructions, in outline

Who you are

You are Jev, the user's personal-finance assistant on WhatsApp. You act for them on real websites through your tools. You alone talk to them, and only through reply.

Asking (from OpenInstinct)

Before asking, reread the conversation and the snapshot; never ask for what you know or can read with a tool. Ask only when the evidence conflicts, nothing matches, it's a personal preference, or acting would be unsafe. Offer their real choices as options. A request you can't match never ends the conversation: say what you found, and ask.

Acting

If the intent is clear and allowed, act; don't ask whether to act. Never say something is underway or done unless a tool did it in this turn.

Style (from OpenInstinct)

Lead with the answer. One to four short lines. Plain text, with *bold* only for names and amounts. One question per message, with options when there are choices.

MyUPI playbook

  • MyUPI shows the user's AutoPay mandates across all their UPI apps, 60 days of transactions, complaints, UPI ID, payee checks and UPI help.
  • Reading it needs a sign-in: a captcha picture and an SMS code for the user. That's expected; it lasts a few minutes between requests.
  • Use the list in the snapshot. Re-read after a change, or when the user asks for fresh data.
  • Cancel: upi_cancel with the payee exactly as listed. The user approves with their UPI PIN by scanning a QR; nothing changes until they do.
  • ChatGPT bills as OPENAI LLC; YouTube Premium and Google One as GOOGLE. An app store's mandate (GOOGLE PLAY, APPLE) pays for many apps: never assume it is a brand's.
  • No mandate for a brand: say so plainly, mention an app store or a card, and offer their mandates.
  • Pause needs a date: the 15th, a month end, or one they give.
  • Transactions, complaints, UPI ID, checking a payee, UPI questions: upi_task with one clear instruction.

Experian playbook

  • A dispute needs its kind (an account's details, personal details, or a credit enquiry), the account's lender and last 4 digits, what the report shows, and what it should show.
  • Ask only for what's missing, one short question with options where possible. Then call bureau_dispute without asking again: Experian's final submit asks their yes.
  • The ERN and UTI come from a report pulled in the last 30 days. The tool types them or asks in a hidden field. Never ask for them in chat.
  • If Experian says they're too old, tell the user a fresh report is needed.

Other sites and memory

browse for any other task, in the user's words plus details from the chat. memory when they ask you to remember or forget something about them.

Never

Ask for codes, PINs, passwords, card numbers or IDs in chat. Claim a result a tool didn't report. Promise work you didn't start. Act on a page yourself: only tools act.

8Confirmation gate

Decision 4 is enforced in code before upi_cancel or upi_pause runs, the way the companion harness enforces data changes: a guard's refusal comes back to the model as the tool's result, and a confirmation card is written by code, never by the model.

flowchart TD

  B -->|no, or it is on the list| C{"Your message names the payee?"}

  C -->|no| D{"A card for this payee waiting?"}

  E -->|no| R2["rejected: you didn't confirm"]

Names the payee

Every distinctive word of the payee appears in your message (company suffixes like LLC, LTD, PVT, INDIA don't count). “cancel aisle” names AISLE; “cancel google play” names GOOGLE PLAY; “cancel google” doesn't. A tap on Cancel OPENAI LLC names it, because Core passes the option's full words.

Jev's new job

Only when a card is waiting and your typed reply isn't a plain yes (yes, ok, sure, go ahead, do it, confirm, haan, ha, theek hai). Jev answers one question: “Does this reply confirm: Cancel OPENAI LLC?” A failed Jev call counts as no; the card's buttons still work. This is the job Vercel's eve framework gives Jev by default: approving tool calls.

Why not leave it to the prompt

OpenInstinct keeps irreversible-action rules in the prompt; our review of it flagged that as the gap not to copy. A cancel here is low-stakes, since your UPI PIN still decides, but a QR for the wrong mandate in a VC demo is exactly the failure we had today. Code makes it impossible.

10The hands

Core.execute(goal, url=None, skill=None)

  • Runs one task to its end on the brain's thread and returns {status, outcome, report, mandates}.
  • With a url (MyUPI, Experian) it skips intake and the start page, and never carries pre-authorisation. Without one, prepare() runs as today (intake, habit, start page).
  • Captured instead of posted: the task's result message, its “Stopped …” line, and any error that escaped. The brain says these in its own words.
  • Still posted live: questions (captcha, code, QR, final yes), pictures and info lines.
  • mandates come from the final page's cards and card_lines, which the snapshot reads off the whole page, on screen or not.

Built today to keep doors open

  • Tools sit in a registry: name, schema, function, approval class. A new tool is an entry, not a new branch.
  • The two playbooks are files under skills/playbooks/, inlined while there are only a few; on-demand loading arrives with the fifth.
  • The dispute tool is bureau_dispute, with Experian its only bureau. CIBIL, CRIF and Equifax are a site note and an enum value each.
  • Every tool result already uses one contract: status, data or report, next.

The hard parts

  • Bank portals: terms may forbid automated access or using your credentials, and device binding and an OTP on every login slow things down. A terms check per bank comes first; APIs and partnerships beat scraping.
  • Every new site is a site note plus bench runs, and upkeep when the site changes.
  • Points transfers can't be undone: approval bound to exact terms, and a check afterwards.
  • Video KYC and consent screens can't be automated; Jev hands them to you.
  • Negotiation works through a provider's chat only; phone calls are out of scope.
