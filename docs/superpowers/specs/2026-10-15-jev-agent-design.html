Jev Agent

Jev does a person's finance tasks end to end on real Indian websites: vouchers, bills, bureau disputes, UPI mandates and card applications. It works in the background and comes back only for what a person must give. Underneath is our own browser agent, written from scratch on browser-use's architecture, with Jev's money guardrails, a per-user vault and a production plan on AWS ECS.

01The promise

The user asks for a task. Jev does it from start to finish in the background, and it asks the user only for the five things below. Everything else is Jev's job.

  • 1
    Secrets not in the vaultOTPs, CVVs, net-banking and wallet logins every time. Site passwords, and card number and expiry once the card vault is on, only until they're saved. A UPI PIN is never typed by Jev at all.
  • 2
    CaptchasShown as a picture sized for a phone. Jev never solves one or pays a service to.
  • 3
    ConsentEvery payment, and every submission that can't be undone or speaks for the user.
  • 4
    A missing personal detailAsked once, then saved in the user's vault.
  • 5
    A clarificationOnly when the request matches several saved targets, or none (two electricity accounts).

Jev decides and does by itself

  • Which store, card or way to pay saves the most
  • Every page, form, banner and pop-up
  • Every retry, a fresh browser or IP, and switching to the next-best option before the payment
  • Learning the path that worked, and reusing it

Never kept anywhere

  • OTPs, CVVs and UPI PINs
  • Net-banking and wallet logins
  • Aadhaar numbers
  • Any secret in a model payload, log or trace. Models see placeholders only.

Targets

WhatTarget
Tasks finished≥ 95 % of started tasks end done. The user's no, a declined card and abandoned tasks don't count against this.
AsksOnly from the ask list. Every ask is counted by kind in the trace, and tests fail on any other.
Purchase with a learned route≤ 60 s agent time (excluding waiting on the user), ≤ $0.02 model spend

Settled in the interviews of 15–16 Oct. Each one names what we chose; the reasons are in the sections below.

03Architecture

Jev is a stack of small layers, each with one job. Work flows down from the chat to the Chrome pool. Page state, effects and questions flow back up. Shared services sit to the side.

LayerDoes
cdp/One websocket per Chrome, commands with ids and timeouts, events routed by session, flattened sessions for cross-origin frames, 3 reconnects. No daemon, no control tab.
browser/One session per task: its browser context (with proxy), its tabs, downloads and permissions, an event bus, and the watchdogs.
dom/Reads the page the way browser-use does: snapshot, DOM and accessibility trees for every frame, covered elements dropped, stable element numbers, Jev's masking.
tools/The action registry and its mechanics. Every action passes execute().
agent/The step loop: routes, model call, actions, effects, loop detector, compaction, judge.
runtime/Durable tasks, asks with expiry, consent, pause and continue, recovery, schedules and bill-due watches.
vault/Per-user encrypted items, scoped use, activity, and the gated card vault.
api/ and brainThe conversation, comparing options and picking the best, and the internal API rivo-companion will call.

04One step of the agent

Each step follows browser-use's loop, with three changes. A learned route can take the step with no model call. What changed on the page, not a screenshot, is the ground truth for each action. A judge must agree before Jev reports success.

PartHow it works
Model output{evaluation_previous_goal, memory, next_goal, plan_update, current_plan_item, facts, actions[≤5]}. Facts follow the job's schema and are grounded on the page.
Action list endsAt a navigation, search, back or tab switch; at any change of URL, focused tab or the set of controls; at an error, an ask or a consent.
EffectsEach action returns what it changed: controls new or gone, URL, values, dialogs, tabs, downloads. A screenshot is added only when these can't confirm the action.
Prompt guidanceIn our own words:
  • clear banners first;
  • for autocomplete, type, then pick the suggestion;
  • redo actions a page change cut short;
  • filters before browsing;
  • a 403 goes to recovery;
  • note what was tried;
  • before done, check every requirement against the page.
Limits
  • 40 steps, plus 10 after the yes.
  • 180 s a step. Per action: click 15 s, type 60 s, navigate 30 s, select 8 s.
  • 5 model retries with backoff. An empty answer is retried once.
Loop detectorActions hashed over a window of 20, pages fingerprinted. Warnings at 5, 8 and 12 repeats, and after 5 unchanged steps. The third warning goes to recovery.
MemoryOne rebuilt state message each step. History over 40,000 characters is summarised to 6,000 or fewer, keeping the last 6 steps, with secrets filtered out.
done and judge
  • done returns the job's schema with an explicit status, never a default.
  • Proof comes first: an order id or a reference on a page after the yes.
  • Then a strict judge on the text history must agree. If it doesn't, one more round. If it still doesn't, the result is "unclear".

05Every action: execute()

Whether the model chose an action or a route replays it, the action takes the same five steps in order. Only two of them can reach the user mid-action.

Action mechanics, browser-use's with our fixes

ActionMechanics
click
  • Scroll into view, then hit-test at the point. The target, an ancestor, a descendant or its label all pass.
  • Mouse move, press and release with short human delays. A checkbox is checked to have toggled.
  • A covered control gets a script click, except a pay or submit button: the model is told what covers it.
  • A new tab is switched to and announced. A download is tracked by its events.
input
  • Focus, then clear through the native setter.
  • Key events per character. Date and datepicker fields are set through their setter.
  • The value is read back (secrets only checked as non-empty), and a reformatted value is reported.
  • Autocomplete gets a hint to wait for its suggestions.
dropdownsNative select, menus, and ARIA listboxes, including options that live elsewhere through aria-controls (browser-use can't). Exact match; on a miss, the options are listed.
extractOur own DOM-to-markdown on a fresh read, in 100,000-character chunks with table headers carried over. The output is masked.
search_page, find_elementsFree and instant, and they also search inside frames.
evaluateRead-only, in an isolated world. Refused on secret pages, payment hosts, checkouts and after the yes. A script that changes the page stops a money job.
ask_user, doneSee §07 and §04.

06Browser and page reader

Most wasted turns come from reading a page before it's ready. In the 17:52 SmartBuy run, 8 of 26 model turns only waited, looked or scrolled. browser-use sleeps 0.3 s while requests are in flight. Jev waits until the page is actually quiet.

Settle stepRule
After navigatingThat navigation's load event, up to 8 s. A timeout is told to the model.
Network quietNo request in flight for 500 ms. Ads, analytics, media, sockets, long-polls and anything older than 10 s don't count.
DOM quietNo mutations for 300 ms, capped at 3 s.
SuggestionsAn autocomplete box waits until its options show.
Empty pageWait 3 s, read again, reload once, then tell the model.

Watchdogs

WatchdogDoes
bannersOur own handler. Rejects optional cookies on OneTrust, Cookiebot, Didomi, Quantcast, TrustArc and in-house banners before the model reads the page.
dialogsReads every dialog and never auto-accepts. An alert is noted. A confirm or prompt waits for the model. A confirm that commits goes to consent.
downloadsEvent-driven, with no fixed sleep after every click. Bills and receipts are attached to the task.
securityAllowed hosts checked before navigation, on redirects and new tabs, with URLs parsed strictly (the CVE-2025-47241 lesson).
crashReopens a crashed tab once, then hands over to recovery. browser-use ships this disabled.
popups, permissions, screenshotNew tabs adopted. Notifications, camera and microphone denied. Captures masked.

Page reader

  • Capture: DOMSnapshot with paint order, plus the DOM and accessibility trees, for every frame. That includes cross-origin frames (depth ≤ 5, ≤ 100 frames) and open or closed shadow roots.
  • Filtering: elements fully covered by opaque ones above are dropped. Interactivity is decided by tag, role, listeners, tabindex and cursor.
  • For the model: one line per element, *[backendNodeId]<role name=… value=… />, with * on new elements and "N pages above, M below", capped at 40,000 characters.
  • Masking: secret fields show [secret], vault values show placeholders, and after the yes codes are masked except amounts and the order id.

08Consent and money

The consent gate sits inside execute(), so no model output can get around it. The summary is written in code from the job and the page, never by a model.

ClassCovers
payPayments, including the per-store card-on-file option
submitA bureau dispute, a card application
cancelCancelling a UPI mandate
acceptConsents or terms on the user's behalf beyond a store's own checkout terms, such as a bureau pull
  • Checks run before the question:
    • the cart is exactly the order;
    • the payable is no more than the amount plus any fee shown;
    • no unsettled purchase or bill in the last 24 hours;
    • the page wasn't reached by typing its address.
  • Binding: the yes is bound to a hash of class, payee, amount, items and method, and any change asks again. The bank's OTP and the gateway steps ride on that yes.
  • No standing approvals: money jobs never use standing or pre-approval.
  • Audit: every consent is recorded with user, time, summary hash and answer. No values.
  • Screenshots are masked and refused on card, OTP and password pages and after the yes. evaluate is fenced the same way.

Card vault launch gate. RBI's card-on-file rules (since Oct 2022) let only card issuers and networks store card data, and PCI DSS 3.3.1 forbids keeping a CVV after a payment. The card vault is built as an isolated service with its own key and role, and answers only card(user, card_id, consent_id) for a live consent. It stays switched off in production (CARD_VAULT=off) until there is a legal opinion and PCI DSS controls are in place. Until then, each store saves the card as an RBI token, and only the CVV and OTP are asked.

10Background tasks

A task is a row in Postgres that survives restarts. It holds the plan, facts, chosen option, route position and consents, never a secret. One money task runs per user at a time; overall, 20 run at once.

Schedules

  • "Pay my electricity bill on the 5th", "buy ₹2,000 of Amazon vouchers monthly"
  • Each run is an ordinary task with a fresh consent. It waits in the chat if the user isn't there.
  • A run missed while the server was down runs once if less than 12 h late.

Bill-due watches

  • Saved billers are read, read-only, weekly, and daily in the 5 days before the due date.
  • A new bill or a nearing due date posts one message with "Pay now".
  • Duplicate alerts are suppressed. A task silent for 3 minutes posts one progress line.

11Recovery

The user's yes is the line. Before it, Jev fixes things by itself and never bothers the user. After it, nothing is ever pressed twice.

FailureWhat Jev does by itself
TransientTimeout, 5xx, network error, crash or empty page: retry twice, reload, then a new context and IP, restarting from the route (≤ 2 restarts).
Blocked403, bot page or 3 captchas in a row: a new context and IP once, then the next-best option. For bills, a retry in an hour with one chat line.
UnavailableOut of stock, limit reached, brand not sold or maintenance: the next-best option.
Bad user detailInvalid account number or login refused: one detail ask, and the vault value is corrected.
Agent stuckLoop detector or 5 failures: astra for 3 steps, one fresh restart, the next-best option, then the user is told what was tried.

12A purchase end to end

"Get me ₹250 of Swiggy vouchers." The user is asked five times: three secrets, one consent and one bank OTP. Each of those needs a person, and nothing else does.

When SmartBuy is down

Retry twice, then a new context and IP. Still down, so Jev switches to Woohoo with UPI. The consent says "SmartBuy wasn't loading, so this is Woohoo with UPI, 3% off with code WOOHOO". After the yes, the QR goes to the chat.

A scheduled bill

On the 5th, a task reads the BESCOM bill, and its captcha waits in the chat. The user types it, then consents to "Pay ₹1,240 to BESCOM · UPI". The QR is checked against ₹1,240 and the payee. The user taps I paid, and Jev shows the receipt and reference.

13Routes: learning the path that worked

A successful run saves its steps up to the yes as a route for that store or biller. Values are saved only as placeholders and variables: {brand}, {amount}, {account}, [vault:…], [ask]. Some steps are never replayed:

  • amounts;
  • steps on pages that show the cart, payable, fee or bill;
  • the yes and everything after it.

Before each model call, the next route step is found by trying these in order:

  1. Element hash: parent tag path, static attributes, accessible name
  2. Stable hash: the same, without changing style class names
  3. XPath
  4. Role and accessible name
  5. A unique name, id or aria-label
  6. No single match: the model takes the step, and the route resumes after the step it took

A newer success replaces the route. A failure before the yes in a run that used the route drops it. On the replica store, the second purchase already signs in and opens the cart with no model calls.

14Deployment on AWS ECS

This is planned now and deployed later. Everything runs in Mumbai, so data stays in India. Postgres is the only queue and state store, with no Redis and no SQS.

ServiceRunsSizeScaling
apiInternal API, in-app chat and vault pages, the brain, ask and consent endpoints1 vCPU · 2 GB2 or more, on CPU
schedulerSchedules, bill-due watches, recovery timers, missed runs0.5 vCPU · 1 GB1 active (Postgres advisory lock), 1 standby

cardvaultThe card vault, flag off in production0.5 vCPU · 1 GB2, isolated security group

How a secret answer reaches the right worker

ConcernPlan
QueueTasks are claimed with SELECT … FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED. Services are woken by LISTEN/NOTIFY, with polling as a backstop. One money task per user is enforced in the claim.
LeasesA heartbeat every 10 s. Three missed heartbeats end the lease: before the yes the task is re-queued; after it, the task is unclear and the user is told.
DeploysRolling at 100 % / 200 %. On SIGTERM a worker stops claiming and re-queues tasks before the yes. Tasks after the yes get the remaining 120 s Fargate stop timeout. Migrations run as a one-off task first.
ImagesWorker: Debian, Chrome stable, Xvfb and Indic fonts. All images run as non-root with a read-only root filesystem, --disable-dev-shm-usage for Chrome, and are scanned in ECR.
SecurityLeast-privilege IAM per service: only the worker decrypts vault keys, with the user's id as context, and the api only encrypts. CloudTrail on every KMS use, GuardDuty and Security Hub. No SSH, and ECS Exec is off in production.
ObservabilityMasked JSON logs. Metrics for tasks by outcome and failure class, asks by kind, agent time, model spend, queue wait and vault use. Alarms when completion drops below 95 % or queue wait exceeds 2 min.
DeliveryTerraform modules for staging and production accounts. GitHub Actions:
  1. tests on every PR;
  2. on main, images built and pushed to ECR;
  3. deploy to staging, then replicas and a read-bench smoke test;
  4. manual approval;
  5. production deploy.
RecoveryRDS point-in-time restore (RPO 5 min), a Terraform rebuild (RTO 1 h), S3 versioning.
Cost driversWorker Fargate hours, residential proxy traffic, model tokens, NAT data, RDS.

17Testing

  • Units: the CDP client against a fake socket, settle against scripted network and DOM activity, the serialiser against recorded snapshots, lookups against fixtures, execute() guards, consent binding, and redaction everywhere a string goes.
  • Replica tests in headless Chrome, with the model scripted. New fixtures:
    • a cross-origin card frame;
    • shadow DOM;
    • a covered pay button;
    • a confirm dialog;
    • a consent banner;
    • a slow XHR page;
    • 500 and 403 pages;
    • an out-of-stock page;
    • a net-banking login.
  • Ask-count assertions: every replica flow asserts its exact ask sequence and nothing more.
  • Data scans: no secret or vault value in the database (outside ciphertext), traces, logs or model payloads after any test.
  • Vault tests:
    • a login only on its host;
    • a card only with a live consent;
    • PAN only on bureau, bank and issuer hosts;
    • ciphertext moved to another user fails;
    • every use appears in Activity.
  • Deploy drills: a rolling deploy mid-task, a worker killed while waiting, an RDS failover, a point-in-time restore.
  • Live: a read-only bench across every store × 5 brands and 5 billers. From stage 3, one real purchase per store changed, by the user.

What we take: watchdogs on an event bus, CDP only, the snapshot reader with paint-order occlusion and stable element numbers, the step output and list endings, the click and typing chains, the dropdown tools, retries, the loop detector, compaction, the action registry, domain-scoped placeholders, chunked extract, free text search, and the five-way replay lookup.

Human-graded, early-2025 browser-use with GPT-4o finished about 30 % of live tasks on Online-Mind2Web. Jev's reliability has to come from settle, effects, recovery, consent and routes, proven on our own bench.
